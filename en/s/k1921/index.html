Asylum: application, interview, refusal
  • You apply for asylum inside the country or at its border. Before applying, gather evidence: case files, summonses, police reports, medical records of injuries, publications, witness statements.
  • Since 12 June 2026 the EU migration pact applies: Russians' approval rate (18% in 2025) is below the 20% threshold, so claims go through an accelerated procedure (up to 12 weeks, often in closed centres near the border).
  • After a refusal you may be sent to a 'safe third country' (such as Serbia, Armenia or Georgia), and an appeal no longer automatically stops removal. Lawyers advise explaining from the start why the countries you travelled through are unsafe for you.
  • At the interview, be consistent and detailed: contradictions with your forms are a common reason for refusal. Human rights groups advise not contacting the Russian consulate or travelling to Russia during the procedure.
  • Where to turn: PRO ASYL and Connection e.V. (Germany), VluchtelingenWerk (Netherlands), inTransit, Reforum Help (designated 'undesirable' in Russia; contact it from a phone that is unlikely to be seized); country-by-country rules: AIDA.
  • On 23 July 2025 Germany stopped issuing humanitarian visas under section 22 of the Residence Act. The fast-track procedure for Russians and Belarusians no longer applies; according to human rights groups, visas are now granted only in a few high-profile cases. What remains is an ordinary asylum claim or a regular visa for work, study or family reunification. The rules keep changing, so check with PRO ASYL.
  • If you survived torture, violence or long pressure, say so at the start of the procedure. Under the new EU rules (Regulation 2024/1348, art. 24, in force since 12 June 2026) the authority may order a free medical examination for signs of persecution; if it does not, you can have one at your own cost. Torture survivors are entitled to treatment and rehabilitation (Directive 2024/1346).
  • A doctor's or psychologist's report under the Istanbul Protocol (the UN standard) helps explain why you mix up dates: trauma affects memory, and the absence of marks does not mean there was no torture. Such reports are made free of charge by: the Bundesverband PSZ network in Germany (XENION in Berlin), Freedom from Torture and the Helen Bamber Foundation in the UK, PHR in the USA, and iMMO in the Netherlands. Almost everywhere a lawyer has to make the request, and waits run to weeks or months.
  • In Germany, a medical certificate about an illness that stands in the way of removal must be submitted without delay and state the diagnosis, the method of examination and the consequences (section 60a of the Residence Act). Not every court accepts certificates from psychotherapists.

Designations of organisations in this guide: Reforum Help (undesirable). Contacting them carries a risk: do not write from a phone that may be seized, and do not repost their materials.

inTransit

Helps anti-war Russians, deserters and people mobilised against their will: evacuation, EU humanitarian visas, temporary housing and support in visa-free countries.

Since October 2025 Germany has effectively closed fast-track humanitarian visas for Russians. Ask which options remain.

The only official bot is @intransithelpbot. It never asks for money or SMS codes.

Other ways to reach them: website · team@intransit.help

Connection e.V.

German organisation supporting conscientious objectors and deserters: asylum advice and advocacy in individual cases.

In 2025-2026 BAMF has been rejecting Russian deserters en masse. Apply for asylum with a lawyer.

Detailed, regularly updated reports on the asylum procedure in each EU country: timelines, detention, appeals, applicants' rights.

Georgian Young Lawyers' Association (GYLA)

One of Georgia's oldest human rights groups: strategic litigation, defence of people detained at protests, ECHR applications.

Since 14 March 2026 its general free legal aid is suspended: GYLA runs in 'crisis mode' and takes only strategic cases. For a free consultation, try the Transparency International Georgia legal centre.

Polskie Forum Migracyjne (Polish Migration Forum)

Free short-term psychological support and therapy for up to 3 months in Russian, Belarusian and Ukrainian, crisis support, PTSD assessment. Helps all migrants in Poland.

Book a psychologist by email or by text message to +48 532 474 389 (the number does not take calls).

Psychosocial centres for refugees and torture survivors (Bundesverband PSZ)

A network of about 50 centres across Germany: free therapy and counselling for survivors of torture, war and political persecution, with interpreters. Some centres prepare psychological reports for asylum procedures, for example XENION in Berlin.

Find a centre by German state on the 'Hilfe vor Ort' page. Waiting lists can be long; in Munich booking opens only in certain months.

Other ways to reach them: in English · XENION (Berlin)

Physicians for Human Rights: Asylum Network

Free medical and psychological evaluations under the Istanbul Protocol for asylum cases in the USA.

Your lawyer makes the request, ideally about 12 weeks before the date the report is needed.

The Ark (Kovcheg)

Legal advice for people who have left (documents, residence permits, visas, asylum, extradition risk), temporary housing, free psychologists, mutual-aid chats by city.

Lawyers via the Telegram bots @kovcheglive_bot and @EmigrantHelpBot; psychologists and housing via forms on the website. The project was set up with support from the Anti-War Committee, designated 'terrorist' in Russia in March 2026; The Ark itself is a 'foreign agent'. Psychologists: 4-week online groups via a form on the website; waiting time from a few days to weeks.

Reforum Help

Free psychologists and lawyers for forced emigrants: migration and visas, taxes, civil law, humanitarian residence; groups and seminars. Psychologists work in Russian and Belarusian.

A Reforum project of the Free Russia Foundation, designated 'undesirable' in Russia. If you travel to Russia, contacting an 'undesirable' organisation or joining its events can lead to prosecution, so check with a lawyer.
