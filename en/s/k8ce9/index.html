Draft What human rights defenders advise

Phone checks
  • With grounds, officers may search you (art. 27.7 CAO), but may seize a phone only with a written record. The law does not expressly require you to unlock it or give a passcode (pending legal review).
  • Since 23 July 2025 the law (257-FZ) allows border guards to inspect 'things and objects'. The law does not mention devices, but the FSB reads it as covering phones and laptops. Refusing to hand a device over is punished with a fine of 5,000 to 7,000 roubles or up to 15 days' arrest (art. 18.7 of the Code of Administrative Offences). The law does not allow them to demand that you unlock it, but in practice they do: agree with a lawyer in advance how to answer.
  • There have been cases over phone contents: 'searching for extremist materials' (art. 13.53 CAO), Telegram 'stars' sent to channels designated as terrorist, donations to Ukrainian medics.
  • Prepare a phone for travel weeks in advance. It should look ordinary: apps, photos, chat history. A completely empty phone may raise questions, but if there was no time to prepare an ordinary one, an empty phone is still better than your main one. Turn off biometrics and use disappearing messages. Remove subscriptions and donation screenshots well in advance, not just before the border. If a case has already been opened against you, don't delete anything without a lawyer. Bank and cloud records remain.
Safety
  • Prepare your travel phone weeks ahead. It should look ordinary.
  • Cloud backups and Telegram restore deleted data, so set up a separate account for the trip.
  • If your phone was checked, assume it may now carry spyware and contact Access Now.
