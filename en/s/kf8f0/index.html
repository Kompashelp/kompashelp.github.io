Draft What human rights defenders advise

I live under Russian occupation: passport, home, conscription, checks
  • Passport. Decree No. 159 of 20 March 2025 required people to get a Russian passport or another Russian status by 10 September 2025, or leave. Without a Russian passport you lose access to pensions, healthcare, jobs, bank accounts and mobile service. People without status are expelled and can be held in a deportation centre. Under a Ukrainian law in force since 16 January 2026, Russian citizenship obtained under occupation is not considered voluntary and does not end Ukrainian citizenship.
  • Home. Homes of people who left go on 'ownerless' lists; since December 2025 they pass to the municipality without a court. Powers of attorney from Ukrainians are refused; a representative needs a Russian passport. Ukraine does not recognise this. Ukraine's Ombudsman advises checking the property is in Ukraine's State Register of Property Rights and keeping documents and photos. Claims to the Council of Europe's Register of Damage (A3.6) go through Diia; human rights groups advise filing after you leave.
  • Conscription. Since 1 January 2026 men aged 18 to 30 can be called for draft procedures all year, including via the Gosuslugi portal. International law forbids conscripting residents of occupied territory, but it happens, and some school leavers are drafted straight away. Men have been detained in the street and threatened with criminal charges to make them sign contracts. By law a contract is voluntary.
  • Children. Since 2025 'Conversations about Important Things' are held in kindergartens from age 3; Ukrainian was removed from the school curriculum on 15 December 2025; Yunarmiya members get better grades. Parents whose children study in Ukrainian online schools are threatened with fines and loss of parental rights; the UN has recorded threats to take children into state custody. Save Ukraine helps families with children leave: 0 800 333 129.
  • What people are jailed for. Transfers for the Ukrainian army, even 1,000 hryvnias (12 years); social media comments (5.5 and 6 years); contacts with Ukrainian 'representatives'. Ukrainian songs and symbols bring fines. In 2025 at least 134 people in the occupied territories were convicted of treason or espionage. First interrogations usually happen without a lawyer. Human rights groups document torture and incommunicado detention.
  • Phone. In the street and at checkpoints people's photos, chats, contacts and Ukrainian banking apps are checked; people have been detained and beaten after such checks. Not having MAX installed may raise suspicion. MAX has no end-to-end encryption and reports VPN use. Human rights groups warn that a 'wiped' phone also raises questions, and deleted data often survives in the cloud and on the other person's phone.
  • Contact with Ukraine. Many Ukrainian rights groups are 'undesirable' in Russia: the Crimean Human Rights Group, the Crimean Tatar Resource Centre, ZMINA, the Center for Civil Liberties, MIHR. Taking part or donating brings a fine, and a repeat a criminal case (art. 284.1). Contact with Ukrainian security services has also led to prosecutions. It is safer for relatives outside occupation to contact Ukrainian bodies. The ICRC works legally in Russia: 8 800 600 92 69.
Safety
  • Now: write down on paper the number of a relative outside the occupied areas and agree what they will do if you disappear.
  • Traces: transfers, chats on the other person's phone, cloud backups and operator records of calls to +380 numbers remain even if you delete everything on your side.
  • Don't: don't send money for the Ukrainian army or to 'undesirable' organisations, and don't write in open chats about the military or your views.

Designations of organisations in this guide: Media Initiative for Human Rights (MIHR) (undesirable); ZMINA Human Rights Centre (undesirable); Center for Civil Liberties (undesirable); Crimean Human Rights Group (undesirable); Crimean Tatar Resource Center (undesirable). Contacting them carries a risk: do not write from a phone that may be seized, and do not repost their materials.

Free help for Ukrainians to leave occupied territories and Russia, where they were taken against their will: route, escort, humanitarian and psychological support.

Save Ukraine

Charity that evacuates children, families and young people from occupied areas, Russia and Belarus, including when the child is there and parents are in Ukraine or Europe; route advice, then shelter, documents and psychological support. Reports 1,763 children rescued.

Работает именно с людьми в оккупации и в России и обещает конфиденциальность. Номер 0 800 бесплатный с украинских номеров. След контакта в телефоне опасен при проверках. Статус в России не найден (независимых сообщений нет).

Ukrainian Parliament Commissioner for Human Rights (Ombudsman)

Ukrainian state body. Staff work permanently at the Domanove crossing; passes lists of detained civilians to the ICRC and the Russian side; works on returns from deportation centres and the Georgian border; takes reports on property seizure; consultation centre on finding children.

Переписка с украинским госорганом на телефоне опасна при проверках. Старая линия 0 800 50 17 20 на сайте отключена, хотя ещё встречается в памятках. Безопаснее, чтобы обращались родственники вне оккупации и России. Госорган Украины; статусов по российским реестрам не найдено.

ICRC Central Tracing Agency

Neutral intermediary: takes family requests to trace missing military and civilians, passes information and family messages. Visits detainees in Ukraine; last visit in Russia was in 2024. Works confidentially.

Самый безопасный канал для тех, кто в России или в оккупации: есть бесплатный российский номер. Гарантий результата нет. В оккупированных частях Херсонской и Запорожской областей МККК не присутствует. Работает в России официально (делегация в Москве, команды в Донецке и Луганске).
