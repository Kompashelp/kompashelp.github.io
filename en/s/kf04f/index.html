I'm being deported to Russia from a third country

The guides below are drafts: a lawyer has not reviewed them yet. This is not legal advice.

Right now

  1. Say at once, orally and in writing, that you are asking for asylum and fear persecution in Russia.
  2. Sign nothing without an interpreter and a lawyer, especially consent to 'voluntary departure'.
  3. Write to Kovcheg and give someone close the decision number, the appeal deadline and where you are held.

If someone close is being deported: find a local lawyer and check the appeal deadline, which can be as short as 7 days.

Draft What human rights defenders advise

I'm being deported to Russia from a third country
  • First, ask for protection. Tell the police or migration service, orally and in writing, that you are asking for asylum (international protection) and fear returning to Russia; name the criminal case or other persecution. Demand a lawyer and an interpreter. Sign nothing without a translation, especially consent to 'voluntary departure': according to AIDA, deportations from Turkey are often recorded as 'voluntary return'.
  • Deportation vs extradition. Extradition answers a Russian request in a criminal case; a court decides it, and in political cases it is usually refused. Deportation or removal is the expulsion of a foreigner for breaking migration rules: overstaying a visa, working without a permit, 'a threat to security'. It moves faster, and courts often don't examine what awaits the person in Russia. An inTransit coordinator calls deportation more dangerous than extradition precisely because of the speed.
  • What happened in 2026. On 4 July Turkey deported Ariadna Litvinova to Russia, where she is accused in St Petersburg of 'discrediting' the army: she had overstayed her visa-free period and spent over a month in a removal centre. In Russia she was sent straight to pre-trial detention. After refusing asylum, Germany has been deporting Russians via Turkey, Georgia, Serbia and Armenia. Since early 2026 Kazakhstan has been extraditing and expelling political émigrés. In June 2026 the UAE handed two men to Russia on an Interpol request; one was accused of taking part in an 'illegal armed group'.
  • Appeal deadlines are short. In Turkey a deportation decision is appealed to an administrative court within 7 days; the appeal suspends removal, but for 'security codes' there is almost no protection (AIDA). In Serbia you have 8 days against a refusal of entry and 15 days against the end of a residence permit or an order to leave. Find out the deadline in your country from a lawyer on the first day and ask for a copy of the decision.
  • Urgent international measures. If removal is imminent, a lawyer can urgently ask the European Court of Human Rights for interim measures under Rule 39: this is possible in Council of Europe countries (Turkey, Georgia, Armenia, Serbia, EU states) without waiting for the final domestic decision. In 2026 the Court used it to stop Georgia expelling activist Nikolai Belikov. In Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan lawyers ask the UN Human Rights Committee for interim measures. Such a measure guarantees nothing: in 2026 Georgia breached one once.
  • UNHCR. Ask detention centre staff to put you in touch with UNHCR (the UN Refugee Agency), or find contacts for your country at help.unhcr.org. UNHCR is not a lawyer, but in some cases it steps in when a person faces removal to where they are persecuted.
  • Leaving for a third country. If you are being expelled for an administrative offence, ask to be allowed to leave for a third country at your own expense: Serbia usually gives a deadline to leave for a country of your choice, and in Georgia this was the outcome in the Kulanin case. Keep a valid international passport and money for a ticket at hand.
  • If you are still being sent to Russia. Tell your family the flight and arrival time so they can arrange a lawyer in Russia in advance. If there is a case against you, you may be detained right at the airport, as Litvinova was. After detention, give no statements without your own lawyer (art. 51 of the Constitution).
  • In advance, before anything happens. Watch your visa and residence permit deadlines, avoid minor offences, keep copies of evidence of persecution with a trusted person outside Russia, and don't go to a Russian consulate without a lawyer. Human rights groups advise people persecuted in Russia to avoid Turkey, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan and Belarus and to choose countries with independent courts.
  • Where to turn: Kovcheg (a 'foreign agent'; lawyers via the @kovcheglive_bot bot), inTransit, First Department (a 'foreign agent') if there is a criminal case or extradition request, UNHCR. In Georgia GYLA, in Armenia HCA Vanadzor, in Kazakhstan the Kazakhstan International Bureau for Human Rights, in Germany PRO ASYL. Be ready for a local lawyer to cost money. This is not legal advice.
Safety
  • Leave copies of your passport, residence permit, migration decisions, case number and flight details with a trusted person outside the country: without them a lawyer can't step in.
  • Don't agree to meet Russian consular staff without a lawyer: the consul is not your defender.
  • Discuss publicity with a lawyer: sometimes it helps stop a deportation, sometimes it gets in the way.

Where you are now

The Ark (Kovcheg)

Legal advice for people who have left (documents, residence permits, visas, asylum, extradition risk), temporary housing, free psychologists, mutual-aid chats by city.

Lawyers via the Telegram bots @kovcheglive_bot and @EmigrantHelpBot; psychologists and housing via forms on the website. Psychologists: 4-week online groups; waiting time from a few days to weeks. The Ark itself is a 'foreign agent'. The project was set up with support from the Anti-War Committee, designated 'terrorist' in Russia since March 2026. The Kovcheg website footer links to the Anti-War Committee: do not follow that link or subscribe to its channels from a device that could be checked.

Other ways to reach them: team@kovcheg.live

Kazakhstan International Bureau for Human Rights and Rule of Law

Protects Russians in Kazakhstan from extradition and deportation and helps with asylum; also takes cases of Kazakh activists and journalists.

Helps people already in Kazakhstan; lawyer Artur Alkhastov advises Russians. Entering or transiting Kazakhstan is risky: since January 2026 the authorities have extradited and deported political refugees.

Other ways to reach them: Telegram channel @kibhr

inTransit

Helps anti-war Russians, deserters and people mobilised against their will: evacuation, EU humanitarian visas, temporary housing and support in visa-free countries.

Since October 2025 Germany has effectively closed fast-track humanitarian visas for Russians. Ask which options remain.

The only official bot is @intransithelpbot. It never asks for money or SMS codes.

Other ways to reach them: website · team@intransit.help

Georgian Young Lawyers' Association (GYLA)

One of Georgia's oldest human rights groups: strategic litigation, defence of people detained at protests, ECHR applications.

Since 14 March 2026 its general free legal aid is suspended: GYLA runs in 'crisis mode' and takes only strategic cases. Staff has been cut and the Batumi office closed. For a free consultation, try the Transparency International Georgia legal centre.
