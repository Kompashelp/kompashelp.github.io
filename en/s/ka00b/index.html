Criminal case: what happens next
  • Now: a suspect can be held for up to 48 hours before a court decides on pre-trial measures. Human rights groups usually advise asking for your own lawyer at once and giving no statement before meeting them (art. 51 of the Constitution).
  • Pre-trial measures range from a written undertaking not to leave and bans on certain actions to house arrest or a pre-trial detention centre (SIZO). The court's ruling can be appealed within 3 days.
  • Next: the investigation usually takes months. Plea deals and confessions are often offered; discuss with a lawyer what a deal would actually give you before agreeing. A lighter sentence is not guaranteed.
  • A verdict can be appealed within 15 days, then in cassation. To complain to the UN Human Rights Committee later you must go through every instance (see the note on international complaints).
  • Where to turn: First Department (foreign agent), Memorial (designated 'extremist' in Russia), the Institute for Law and Public Policy (foreign agent); about life in SIZO: Prison Lawyer; letters and parcels: Solidarity Zone (foreign agent). OVD-Info ('extremist' in Russia) advises but no longer refers lawyers inside Russia. Contact organisations designated 'extremist' from a different phone from the one that could be seized.
  • Your own lawyer. Your family can hire a lawyer for you: other people may engage a defender with your consent (art. 50 CPC). The lawyer joins the case by showing their ID and warrant (art. 49 CPC). If the lawyer you asked for has not come within 5 days, the investigator may suggest another and then appoint one (art. 50 CPC).
  • The prosecutor supervises the investigation, approves the indictment and argues the case in court (art. 37 CPC). The prosecutor must answer a complaint about the investigator within 3 days, or 10 in exceptional cases (art. 124 CPC). Actions violating your rights can be challenged in the district court, which rules within 14 days (art. 125 CPC). First Department notes that complaints do not always help but record violations.
  • Statements and your lawyer. A statement made without a lawyer and not confirmed in court is inadmissible (art. 75 CPC). But what you say with a lawyer present can be used even if you retract it (arts 46, 47 CPC). Before the first questioning you may talk to a lawyer alone (art. 46 CPC). A confession without other evidence is not enough to convict (art. 77 CPC).
  • If the charge is financing terrorism (art. 205.1 CC). The case is heard by a district military court, a panel of three judges, with no jury (arts. 30, 31 CPC). There is no simplified procedure (art. 314 CPC). The law does not allow a term below the minimum or a suspended sentence (arts. 64, 73 CC). Cases under art. 282.3 are heard by a district court.
  • Property. An arrest in the case (art. 115 CPC) secures a fine or confiscation. It is not yet a seizure. A sole home without a mortgage may not be arrested (art. 115 part 4 CPC, art. 446 Civil Procedure Code). In donation cases courts usually confiscate the donated sum and the phone. We found no case where a donor's flat was confiscated.
  • Family money. If the case is under an 'extremist' or 'terrorist' article (for example, a donation to FBK) or under art. 280.3 CC ('discrediting' the army), the accused is put on the Rosfinmonitoring list before any verdict and his accounts are frozen (Law 115-FZ, art. 6, para 2.1). The accounts of his wife and other relatives are not frozen for that reason alone. If all the family's money sat on the accused's cards, the family can be left without funds (Radio Svoboda, 2025). See 'I'm on the Rosfinmonitoring list'.
Safety
  • Keep all messages about your lawyer and the case on one password-protected device.
  • Think about which chats you share with other people in the case, and discuss with your lawyer whether to leave them.
  • Don't delete anything without your lawyer.
  • Do not discuss your case or your views with cellmates. First Department describes cases where an informer was placed in the cell or a cellmate was forced to give false evidence.
  • If an appointed lawyer asks you or your family for money, that is a warning sign: the state pays them (art. 50 CPC).
  • Relatives should send complaints about a prisoner's treatment only after agreeing this with the prisoner or their lawyer (First Department).
  • If a sole home without a mortgage was arrested, discuss a complaint with the lawyer: the law forbids such an arrest (art. 115 part 4 CPC).
  • Keep a copy of the decision to charge: it shows which transfers are charged and under which article.
