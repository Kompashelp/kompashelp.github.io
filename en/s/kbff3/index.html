Pressure on children and parents: school, university, child services
  • Children are not taken away over their own or their parents' views alone, but a complaint from school can turn into a child services check or the family being put on a police watch list.
  • Ask child services staff for ID and call their office to check. By law, without police and a registered crime report you do not have to let them in. Rights groups advise filming the visit and calmly saying you are recording. If filming makes things tense, note names, positions and the time instead.
  • The inspection report must be given in writing within 10 days and can be challenged in court. If nothing was found, by law there should be no repeat checks, but in practice they happen.
  • A school cannot require you to install MAX: the digital and education ministries call it voluntary. Write your refusal to the head teacher and ask for another way to stay in touch.
  • OVD-Info has guides on teenagers on police watch lists and on expulsion over protests. OVD-Info is designated 'extremist' in Russia. It says that asking for advice is not taking part in its activities, but contact it from a device you will not have to hand over if detained. Parents need support too: First Line offers anonymous sessions.
  • If a parent is remanded in custody, by law the investigator must hand over children left without care to relatives or other people, or, if there is nobody, to a children's institution (art. 160 CPC). Tell the investigator and the lawyer straight away who can take the children. Parents can file a joint application with child services in advance, naming a temporary guardian for the period when they cannot look after the child (art. 13 of Law 48-FZ).
  • You do not have to tell the school the details of a parent's case. If the child is bullied or pressured, write down what happened and complain to the head teacher in writing. Families of political prisoners get help from Freedom Zone and Memorial Political Prisoners Support (designated 'extremist' in Russia; contact it from a phone that is unlikely to be seized). Psychologists at First Line and Nora help both children and adults.

Whom to contact

3 organisations

First Line

Free, anonymous psychological support for people affected by, or afraid of, political persecution and for their families; an information line and safety guides.

Psychological help is in a separate bot, @PsyFirstlineBot: free, anonymous, one-off chat sessions with trained volunteer psychologists, 18+, no diagnoses or medication. Confidentiality is broken if there is a risk of suicide or a threat to a child.

The only official bot is @FirstLineHelpBot. It never asks for money or SMS codes.

Other ways to reach them: website · psychology bot

First Department

Lawyers for treason, espionage, state secrets and extremism cases, including closed trials. Advice on digital security.

The only official bot is @deptonebot. It never asks for money or SMS codes.

Other ways to reach them: website · info@dept.one

OVD-Info

Advice for people who are detained or face administrative or criminal cases over protests and anti-war statements. They can tell you how to behave during questioning.

Since June 2026 it no longer works with lawyers in Russia; it gives advice and refers people onward. For criminal cases and pressure on families, also contact First Department and Prison Lawyer. The 8 800 hotline is switched off; only the bot works. Since 4 June 2026 it is on the Rosfinmonitoring list as 'extremist': do not repost its materials.

The only official bot is @ovdinfobot. It never asks for money or SMS codes.
