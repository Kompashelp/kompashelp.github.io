Persecuted in Georgia: protests and 'foreign agents'
  • Blocking a road: a 5,000 lari fine and up to 60 days' detention; with unpaid fines, a repeat offence can mean jail. Rights groups advise appealing every ruling and keeping video.
  • Beatings during arrests are common. Record injuries at once: doctor, photos, complaint. This is the basis for a future ECHR case.
  • An 'unauthorised' foreign grant can mean up to 6 years in prison, applied retroactively; 12 NGOs have had accounts frozen. Lawyers advise reviewing all of an organisation's grants in advance.
  • Foreigners, including Russians, risk expulsion for taking part in protests, and for some that means being sent to Russia.
  • Where to turn: the Transparency International Georgia legal centre, GYLA (strategic cases), Human Rights Center; human rights defenders: Front Line Defenders.

current as of 25 September 2026. More: HRW: new laws devastate civic groups; HRW: laws that criminalise peaceful protest

Georgian Young Lawyers' Association (GYLA)

One of Georgia's oldest human rights groups: strategic litigation, defence of people detained at protests, ECHR applications.

Since 14 March 2026 its general free legal aid is suspended: GYLA runs in 'crisis mode' and takes only strategic cases. For a free consultation, try the Transparency International Georgia legal centre.

Transparency International Georgia

Free legal consultations at its legal centres in Tbilisi, Kutaisi, Batumi and Zugdidi; monitors the prosecution of protesters and NGOs.

The legal centre advises members of the public; ask whether they take foreigners' cases.
