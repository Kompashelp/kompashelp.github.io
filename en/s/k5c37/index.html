Read first

If you are in Belarus or carry a device that may be checked: do not open or subscribe to these organisations' sites and channels: it can be used to open a case. Save the contacts in advance and open them from a device you will not take across the border and that is not checked.

The guides below are drafts: a lawyer has not reviewed them yet. This is not legal advice.

Right now

  1. Find out: 'preventive talk' or interrogation? At an interrogation you may have a lawyer.
  2. Say as little as possible: 'yes', 'no', 'I don't know'.
  3. Don't name people, chats or contacts of those who left.

Summoned by the KGB or police for a 'talk'
  • First find out what it is: a 'preventive talk' or an interrogation. At an interrogation you have a procedural status and the right to a lawyer.
  • Viasna's lawyers advise saying as little as possible ('yes', 'no', 'I don't know') and not being drawn into a 'friendly' conversation.
  • Don't name people, chats or contacts of those who left. Expect your phone and Telegram to be inspected or photographed.
  • Sign the record of the talk only after reading it in full; you can state your disagreement with its wording in writing.
  • Discuss an offer to 'cooperate' with human rights defenders at once, but contact them from a device that is not checked. Note that contact with an 'extremist formation' from inside Belarus can in itself become grounds for a case. If you signed a non-disclosure pledge, discuss it with a lawyer once you are abroad. The recruitment attempt can later support an asylum claim.

Designations of organisations in this guide: Viasna Human Rights Centre (extremist in Belarus). Contacting them carries a risk: do not write from a phone that may be seized, and do not repost their materials.
