Travelling back to Russia: assessing the risks
  • Check yourself: a criminal-record certificate via Gosuslugi, the police wanted list, the bailiffs' database, the Rosfinmonitoring list, the draft register, court websites. More in 'Am I on a list?'. Note that the authorities can see when you log in to Gosuslugi.
  • Think about what could be used against you: donations to 'undesirable' or 'extremist' organisations or the Ukrainian army, posts with banned symbols, subscriptions to banned channels.
  • You can be named a suspect at any moment, including at the border. Human rights groups advise travelling with an ordinary phone prepared in advance, with a safe history, and arranging contact with a lawyer beforehand. Agree a check-in call with someone outside Russia.
  • At the border you'll be asked about your trip, job, relatives in Ukraine and view of the war, and devices may be inspected. If detained, ask your status, demand a lawyer, invoke art. 51 of the Constitution.
  • Men of conscription age: if there is a summons in the register, by law you may not leave from the day it is posted. In practice the ban sometimes appears right at passport control, and in September 2026 a reservist was stopped from leaving for the first time, on an enlistment office decision. Check your extract from the summons register before buying tickets. Get advice before the trip: First Department (foreign agent), Network Freedoms, OVD-Info (bot). OVD-Info is designated 'extremist' in Russia; contact it from a device you will not take across the border.
  • Law 257-FZ allows inspection of 'things and objects'. The FSB reads this as a right to inspect phones. Refusing to present a device can bring a fine or up to 15 days' arrest, but the law does not oblige you to unlock it. A 'talk' in a separate room has no time limit: allow many hours for the border.
  • A clean check does not mean there is no case. A criminal record certificate is only accurate on the day it is issued, the public police wanted database lists only people the police chose to search for publicly, and cases reach court websites with a delay.

What traces remain

  • The police wanted database sends its answer by email. Verstka advises giving a spare, empty mailbox and not running the check from your own IP address.
Safety
  • Don't: take your main phone with subscriptions, likes, screenshots and banking apps that show donations. If you cannot do without it, prepare it well in advance, not just before the border.
  • Traces: the authorities can see Gosuslugi logins. If you sign in to your main Telegram, Google or Apple account on a travel phone, your subscriptions, chats, photos and history come back with it.
  • If doubts remain after checking, it is safer not to go. Don't plan your way back through Belarus.

Designations of organisations in this guide: OVD-Info (foreign agent, extremist, on Rosfinmonitoring list). Contacting them carries a risk: do not write from a phone that may be seized, and do not repost their materials.
