Right now

  1. Give no statement without a lawyer: art. 51 protects you from self-incrimination.
  2. Ask in writing for your own lawyer to be admitted.
  3. Summoned as a witness? Bring a lawyer; decline specific questions, not all.

If someone close was detained: find out where they are and tell a lawyer.

Draft What human rights defenders advise

Detained on the street or at a protest
  • Ask on what grounds you are being held and in what capacity. Administrative detention usually lasts up to 3 hours; for offences that can carry arrest (19.3, 20.2 pt 6.1, 20.3 CAO) up to 48 hours (art. 27.5 CAO).
  • Human rights groups usually advise not giving statements: art. 51 of the Constitution lets you refuse to testify against yourself. Access to your own lawyer is requested in writing.
  • Read every record in full, strike through blank fields and write any disagreement under 'explanations'. Ask for a copy of the detention record at once; in a criminal case it is your right (art. 46 CPC). If you get no copy, OVD-Info advises signing with the note 'copy not received'. Don't sign blank forms.
  • By law a phone may be seized only with a written record, and the law does not expressly require you to give your passcode. In practice detainees are sometimes pressured to unlock their phones, so decide with a lawyer in advance how to handle it. Keep your lawyer's and family's numbers on paper.
  • Where to turn: Network Freedoms (@nfp_hotline), First Department, Prison Lawyer. OVD-Info advises via its bot but no longer refers people to lawyers inside Russia. OVD-Info is designated 'extremist' in Russia. It says that asking for advice is not taking part in its activities, but contact it from a device you will not have to hand over if detained. Don't call hotlines from your main SIM card: the call stays in the operator's records.
  • Injuries. Ask for an ambulance and for your injuries to be written into the record. The temporary detention centre (IVS) does a medical check on arrival: ask for your copy of the report. An emergency clinic reports injuries to the police, but get treatment anyway. Details: 'Doctors and police'.
  • An emergency clinic tells the police within a day about bruises, contusions and concussion, giving your name, address and the likely cause (order 664n). Get treatment anyway. OVD-Info advises recording the circumstances in your own words; you don't need to discuss your views.
  • Names and a phone call. A police officer must state their position, rank and surname and show their ID on request (Police Act, art. 5). A detainee may make one call to family within 3 hours (Police Act, art. 14; art. 96 CPC). In a criminal case the investigator, with the prosecutor's consent, may withhold notice of the detention to keep it secret (art. 96 CPC).
  • Copies and forms. Ask for a copy of the detention record: in administrative cases it is given on request (art. 27.4 CAO), in criminal cases it is your right (art. 46 CPC). If you get no copy, OVD-Info advises signing the original with the note 'copy not received'. Do not sign blank forms or empty sheets. You do not have to write an 'explanatory note' (OVD-Info).
Safety
  • Keep your lawyer's and family's numbers on paper.
  • Lock your phone with a long passcode in advance and turn off face and fingerprint unlock.
  • Contact organisations designated 'extremist' from a different phone from the one that could be seized.
  • Give copies of the IVS injury report and clinic notes to your lawyer or family; don't keep them only on a phone that could be seized.
  • Do not discuss your case or views in the cell: informers are placed among detainees (First Department).
  • If your lawyer is not let in, make a written request to the officer drawing up the record, or file a statement with the duty unit and take the registration slip (OVD-Info).

Net Freedoms Project

Legal hotline: cases over posts, reposts and comments, website blocking, detentions via facial recognition.

Set up by lawyers from the Agora human rights group, which Russia designated 'undesirable' in 2023.

The only official bot is @nfp_hotline. It never asks for money or SMS codes.

Nora

Help for activists, volunteers, NGO and media staff, political prisoners and their families: two free sessions with a psychologist or psychiatrist (sometimes more) on application via the website, a peer-support bot run by volunteers, spaces in Moscow and St Petersburg.

They reply within 10 days by email, Signal or Telegram. Choose Signal for the reply. The bot @nora_psyhelp_bot offers peer support, not a psychologist.

The only official bot is @nora_psyhelp_bot. It never asks for money or SMS codes.

OVD-Info

Advice for people who are detained or face administrative or criminal cases over protests and anti-war statements. They can tell you how to behave during questioning.

Since June 2026 it no longer works with lawyers in Russia; it gives advice and refers people onward. For criminal cases and pressure on families, also contact First Department and Prison Lawyer.
