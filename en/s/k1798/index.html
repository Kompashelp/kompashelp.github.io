Draft What human rights defenders advise

Prosecuted for a post, repost or comment
  • Human rights groups usually advise making profiles private, not discussing the war with strangers, avoiding VK and Russian services for anything sensitive, and remembering art. 51 of the Constitution.
  • Old posts are risky too: courts treat them as a 'continuing' offence and count the limitation period from when the post is found. Deleting a post already recorded won't help, but it lowers the risk of new cases.
  • If a report is being drawn up: detention lasts up to 3 hours; ask for a linguistic expert review, note violations in the report, strike out blank lines and take copies.
  • Main offences: 'discrediting the army' (art. 20.3.3 CAO; a repeat within a year becomes criminal, art. 280.3 CC), army 'fakes' (art. 207.3 CC), 'extremist symbols' (art. 20.3 CAO), 'justifying terrorism' (art. 205.2 CC).
  • Where to turn: Network Freedoms (@nfp_hotline), First Department (foreign agent), OVD-Info (bot; 'extremist' in Russia, contact it from a device you will not have to hand over if detained). RosShtraf helps pay fines in anti-war cases.
  • You can be punished for what was legal when you posted it. Once an organisation is designated 'extremist', 'terrorist' or 'undesirable', its logo, a repost or a link in an old post can bring a fine, and a repeat a criminal case. This happened with FBK and the 'LGBT movement'; since June 2026 OVD-Info is 'extremist' too. Human rights groups advise removing such posts early, before any case.
  • A like rarely leads to a case, but it happens: people were fined for likes on Telegram, VKontakte and YouTube, and an Odnoklassniki 'like' counts as a repost. Paid Telegram reactions ('stars') count as money transfers and have led to prison terms. Since September 2025, searching for 'extremist materials' is fined 3,000 to 5,000 rubles; such searches were found in phone checks. A VPN itself is not an offence, but committing a crime through one is an aggravating factor (art. 63 CC).
  • Private talk and chats are not a shield. People were fined for a video forwarded to local chats, a WhatsApp status, an argument on a train and shouting from a window; criminal cases followed a message in a building chat. It often starts with a denunciation by a neighbour, colleague or a follower of a closed account. Human rights groups advise treating large open-to-join chats as public.
  • Money is riskier than words. A donation of any size to an 'extremist' organisation (FBK, OVD-Info) is punishable, one to an 'undesirable' one is criminal at once, one to a Ukrainian fund is treated as treason. Transfers are found via Rosfinmonitoring and phone checks at the border. If you left Russia with an unpaid 'discreditation' fine, an August 2026 law allows frozen money and blocked banking apps.
  • The law restricting people who have left Russia is not only about 'discrediting the army': a fine under Article 20.33 for 'participation' in an 'undesirable' organisation, such as a repost, is also on the list of grounds. If the fine is unpaid, restrictions may follow on banking apps, property transactions, Gosuslugi and consular services.
Safety
  • Make profiles private and remove old posts in advance, while there is no case.
  • Don't use VK or other Russian services for anything sensitive.
  • Don't forward material from designated organisations, even in private messages.
  • If a protocol or case already exists, deleting the post will not undo it: the police already have screenshots. Don't delete anything until you have talked to a lawyer.
  • Don't send paid reactions or money to channels and organisations designated 'extremist', 'terrorist' or 'undesirable', or to Ukrainian funds, even small amounts.
  • Don't search for banned material through Russian search engines or browsers; human rights groups advise a reliable VPN and turning off search history.
  • Before travelling to Russia or Belarus, clear subscriptions, likes, screenshots and banking apps with traces of donations from your phone, or better, take an ordinary phone with a safe history, prepared in advance.

Designations of organisations in this guide: OVD-Info (foreign agent, extremist, on Rosfinmonitoring list). Contacting them carries a risk: do not write from a phone that may be seized, and do not repost their materials.

Legal hotline: cases over posts, reposts and comments, website blocking, detentions via facial recognition.

Set up by lawyers from the Agora human rights group, which Russia designated 'undesirable' in 2023.

Media Defence

A UK organisation gives free legal defence help to journalists, bloggers, photographers, fact-checkers and outlets prosecuted or sued over their reporting. It pays for or finds a lawyer and takes cases up to the European Court of Human Rights and the UN.

Legal help only, no living costs. The journalist, their lawyer, family or a support organisation can apply; the form is available in Russian. If you have no lawyer yet, write to info@mediadefence.org first.

Other ways to reach them: info@mediadefence.org

OVD-Info

Advice for people who are detained or face administrative or criminal cases over protests and anti-war statements. They can tell you how to behave during questioning.

Since June 2026 it no longer works with lawyers in Russia; it gives advice and refers people onward. For criminal cases and pressure on families, also contact First Department and Prison Lawyer. The 8 800 hotline is switched off; only the bot works. Since 4 June 2026 it is on the Rosfinmonitoring list as 'extremist': do not repost its materials.

The only official bot is @ovdinfobot. It never asks for money or SMS codes.
