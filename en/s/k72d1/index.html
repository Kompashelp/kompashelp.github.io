Ukrainians: leaving Russia and occupied territories
  • Helping to Leave volunteers help people leave occupied territories, or Russia where they were taken against their will, for free: bot @helpingtoleave_bot, hotline +380 93 177 64 58. A call to a Ukrainian number from a Russian SIM card stays in the operator's records.
  • Exit checks can be harsh: human rights groups advise removing photos and chats linked to the army or Ukrainian symbols from your phone beforehand and not carrying extra devices.
  • For Ukrainians staying in Russia, the Civic Assistance Committee helps with documents, refugee status and temporary asylum.
  • Russians who want to help Ukrainian prisoners of war or detainees: the prison service sees letters and transfers. Any financial help linked to Ukraine, including help to prisoners of war, can be treated as high treason (art. 275 CC, up to life imprisonment). Talk to First Department before any transfer. Sign letters with your own name and write only about everyday things. Don't send money to people on the 'terrorist' list.
  • The Kolotilovka-Pokrovka crossing has been closed since August 2024. The only humanitarian corridor from occupation into Ukraine now runs through Russia and Belarus to the Mokrany-Domanove crossing (Volyn region), entry only, on foot. Ombudsman staff, Caritas Ukraine and Helping to Leave meet people there. If you are being expelled or held in a temporary detention centre for foreign nationals (TsVSIG), contact the Civic Assistance Committee.
  • Many Ukrainian human rights groups are 'undesirable' in Russia: the Crimean Human Rights Group, the Crimean Tatar Resource Center, ZMINA, the Center for Civil Liberties, MIHR (Media Initiative for Human Rights), Rubikus. Information on political prisoners in Crimea is collected by the Crimean Human Rights Group and the Crimean Tatar Resource Center. Taking part in the work of an 'undesirable' organisation is fined (art. 20.33 CAO), and a repeat means a criminal case; a donation to such an organisation is a criminal case straight away (art. 284.1 CC). So it is better to contact them through relatives outside Russia.
  • Searching for a detained civilian or a deported child: from Russia the safest channel is the ICRC, 8 800 600 92 69 (free). Relatives in Ukraine or abroad file a statement with Ukraine's police and report to the National Information Bureau (1648) and the Coordination Headquarters (0 800 300 529).
Safety
  • Under occupation phones are checked in the street and at checkpoints: the UN reports detentions after Ukrainian banking apps were found.
  • Ukrainian citizens in Russia who send money to funds supporting the Ukrainian army face art. 276.1 ('aiding the enemy', at least 10 years); the first sentence was handed down on 16 September 2026.
  • The Ombudsman's old line 0 800 50 17 20 is disconnected. The current line is 1678, from abroad +380 44 299 74 08.

Designations of organisations in this guide: Rubikus.helpUA (Rubikus e.V.) (undesirable); Media Initiative for Human Rights (MIHR) (undesirable); ZMINA Human Rights Centre (undesirable); Center for Civil Liberties (undesirable); Crimean Human Rights Group (undesirable); Crimean Tatar Resource Center (undesirable). Contacting them carries a risk: do not write from a phone that may be seized, and do not repost their materials.

Helping to Leave

Free help for Ukrainians to leave occupied territories and Russia, where they were taken against their will: route, escort, humanitarian and psychological support.

A call to the +380 number from a Russian SIM card stays in the operator's records.

The only official bot is @helpingtoleave_bot. It never asks for money or SMS codes.

Mission of the President of Ukraine in the Autonomous Republic of Crimea

Ukrainian state body for Crimea: map of places where political prisoners are held, letters project, admission and documents for Crimeans, safety guide (August 2026).

A Ukrainian state body: contact from Crimea is risky. According to First Department, about 10% of treason and espionage cases in 2025 were linked to messages or alleged contacts with Ukrainian intelligence services. No status found.

Other ways to reach them: 0 800 502 192 · 044 293 36 36 · ark@ppu.gov.ua (a call stays in the phone log and with the operator)

Ukrainian Helsinki Human Rights Union (UHHRU)

Free legal help through a network of advice centres across Ukraine, many of which consult remotely. They help people affected by the war: claims to the Register of Damage and court cases for families of missing persons.

Calling a Ukrainian number from occupied areas or from Russia is risky: better contact them after you leave. The website and consultations are in Ukrainian. They have no separate programme for civilians held in Russia.

Other ways to reach them: regional advice centres · Kyiv advice centre +38 044 383 95 19 (a call stays in the phone log and with the operator)

ZMINA Human Rights Centre

Documents persecution in the occupied territories and Crimea, analyses collaboration cases, advocates for civilians in captivity. No individual hotline.

'Undesirable': contact from Russia or the occupied territories carries a risk under CAO art. 20.33 and art. 284.1 of the Criminal Code. This is a data source for the editors, not a help service. OVD-Info, 08.11.2024.

Other ways to reach them: +38 067 502 08 01 (a call stays in the phone log and with the operator)

Center for Civil Liberties

2022 Nobel Peace Prize. Campaigns for the release of civilians, keeps a map of enforced disappearances and a guide for relatives of civilian hostages; runs the People First campaign with Memorial.

'Undesirable'. You can read its guide for reference, but some phone numbers in it are outdated: check numbers against official websites. OVD-Info, 17.04.2024.

undesirable checked against its site 26 September 2026
Get in touch → mailto:office@ccl.org.ua

Crimean Human Rights Group

Documents violations in Crimea and southern Ukraine: political cases, disappearances, conscription, fines for speech; quarterly and annual reviews. No direct help to families described on the site.

'Undesirable' since 2022: messages, passing on materials and reposts from Crimea or Russia carry a risk. The Russian version of the website has not been updated since February 2025; on Kompas's 'Political prisoners' page the link leads to /ru/. Radio Svoboda, 06.05.2022.

Other ways to reach them: +38 067 224 01 73 · crimeahrg@gmail.com (a call stays in the phone log and with the operator)

Crimean Tatar Resource Center

Keeps count of Crimean political prisoners, documents violations, mobilises legal, social and humanitarian aid for prisoners and families, submits to the UN.

'Undesirable' since February 2024. For families in Crimea any contact or donation carries a risk. It is safer for families if relatives outside Crimea and Russia contact the centre. OVD-Info 06.02.2024, Krym.Realii 07.02.2024.
