Ukrainians: leaving Russia and occupied territories
  • Helping to Leave volunteers help people leave occupied territories, or Russia where they were taken against their will, for free: bot @helpingtoleave_bot, hotline +380 93 177 64 58. A call to a Ukrainian number from a Russian SIM card stays in the operator's records.
  • Exit checks can be harsh: human rights groups advise removing photos and chats linked to the army or Ukrainian symbols from your phone beforehand and not carrying extra devices.
  • For Ukrainians staying in Russia, the Civic Assistance Committee helps with documents, refugee status and temporary asylum.
  • Russians who want to help Ukrainian prisoners of war or detainees: the prison service sees letters and transfers. Any financial help linked to Ukraine, including help to prisoners of war, can be treated as high treason (art. 275 CC, up to life imprisonment). Talk to First Department before any transfer. Sign letters with your own name and write only about everyday things. Don't send money to people on the 'terrorist' list.
  • Crimea: data on political prisoners is collected by the Crimean Human Rights Group and the Crimean Tatar Resource Center. Both are 'undesirable' in Russia: from Crimea it is safer to contact them through relatives outside Russia.
  • The Kolotilovka–Pokrovka crossing has been closed since August 2024. The only humanitarian corridor from occupation into Ukraine now runs through Russia and Belarus to the Mokrany–Domanove crossing (Volyn region), entry only, on foot. Ombudsman staff, Caritas Ukraine and Helping to Leave meet people there. If you are being expelled or held in a deportation centre (TsVSIG), contact the Civic Assistance Committee.
  • Many Ukrainian human rights groups are 'undesirable' in Russia: the Crimean Human Rights Group, the Crimean Tatar Resource Centre, ZMINA, the Center for Civil Liberties, MIHR, Rubikus. Taking part or donating brings a fine (art. 20.33 of the Administrative Code), and a repeat a criminal case (art. 284.1). Human rights groups advise that relatives outside Russia contact them.
  • Searching for a detained civilian or a deported child: from Russia the safest channel is the ICRC, 8 800 600 92 69 (free). Relatives in Ukraine or abroad file a statement with Ukraine's police and report to the National Information Bureau (1648) and the Coordination Headquarters (0 800 300 529).
Safety
  • Under occupation phones are checked in the street and at checkpoints: the UN reports detentions after Ukrainian banking apps were found.
  • Ukrainian citizens in Russia who send money to funds supporting the Ukrainian army face art. 276.1 ('aiding the enemy', at least 10 years); the first sentence was handed down on 16 September 2026.
  • The Ombudsman's old line 0 800 50 17 20 is disconnected. The current line is 1678, from abroad +380 44 299 74 08.

Designations of organisations in this guide: Rubikus.helpUA (Rubikus e.V.) (undesirable); Media Initiative for Human Rights (MIHR) (undesirable); ZMINA Human Rights Centre (undesirable); Center for Civil Liberties (undesirable); Crimean Human Rights Group (undesirable); Crimean Tatar Resource Center (undesirable). Contacting them carries a risk: do not write from a phone that may be seized, and do not repost their materials.
