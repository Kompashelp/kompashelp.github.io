Your family is pressured while you're abroad
  • What happens. According to the UN Special Rapporteur on Russia (September 2026), pressure on relatives has become a common way to silence people who left or make them come back. Police, the FSB or Centre 'E' visit parents; in Belarus, the KGB and GUBOPiK. There are 'chats', summonses to questioning as witnesses, searches, seized devices and threats of cases against the relatives themselves. According to OVD-Info, since 2022 relatives have been made to call those who left and ask them to return, or to record apology videos.
  • Relatives' rights. By law no one has to testify against themselves, their spouse or close relatives: art. 51 of the Constitution and art. 56 of the Code of Criminal Procedure in Russia, art. 27 of the Constitution in Belarus. Close relatives under Russian law: spouse, parents, children, full brothers and sisters, grandparents, grandchildren. A 'chat' has no basis in law and can be declined, but what is said may be recorded. Questioning requires a summons, and a lawyer can come along. In practice refusing to talk sometimes angers officers, so decide with a lawyer in advance how to refuse.
  • Searches and papers. A search in a case against someone who left most often takes place at their registered address: if you are registered at your parents' flat, officers will go there. Don't keep your documents or devices at your relatives' homes. Relatives are better off signing nothing they haven't read in full and writing any disagreement straight into the record. By law devices are seized with a written record; ask for a copy.
  • Property. Since 1 September 2026 (Law 168-FZ) a court can freeze the property of someone abroad under certain 'political' administrative articles. By law property that is legally your relatives' is not covered, but First Department lawyers see a risk for property bought during a marriage and gifts to relatives made after a case began. Details: 'My flat or accounts were arrested'.
  • What you can do. Don't come back because of promises to 'just talk': they have no legal force, and in Belarus even people on short visits were detained. Don't tell relatives about your plans or address by phone or in chats. Agree a code word in case they are made to call you. Note dates, names and agencies for your lawyer and an asylum claim.
  • The Caucasus, Central Asia and where to turn. In Chechnya relatives of critics have been abducted and held for weeks, made to disown them publicly, and in 2025 families were expelled from the republic (see 'Fleeing the North Caucasus'). If you are from Uzbekistan, Tajikistan or Turkmenistan, see the situation on pressure on families in Central Asia. Where to turn: First Department (foreign agent), Network Freedoms, and First Line psychologists for relatives. For Belarusians: Viasna ('extremist' in Belarus; contacting it from Belarus is dangerous). OVD-Info is designated 'extremist' in Russia: relatives in Russia should not write to it or donate; you can write from abroad instead.
  • Remarks on the record. OVD-Info advises writing remarks on separate sheets, as many as needed, and noting in the record how many sheets there are and the first and last words. Cross out blank spaces. Don't sign a clause agreeing to be notified by text message. A relative who agrees to talk should know their testimony remains evidence even if they later retract it (art. 56 CCP).
  • Summonses. A summons to questioning for someone abroad can be handed to an adult family member, who is legally obliged to pass it on (art. 188 CCP; Belarus art. 216). Relatives cannot accept a military summons for the person; it counts as served 7 days after it appears in the summons register (art. 31, Law on Military Duty).
  • Non-disclosure undertaking. After signing it, relatives must not pass on case details, including to you (art. 310 Criminal Code; Belarus art. 407). In Russia, reporting violations by officials and writing complaints is allowed (art. 161 CCP). Ask the investigator to write down exactly what may not be disclosed.
Safety
  • Don't come back, even 'briefly', because of officers' promises until you have talked to a lawyer: people who returned have been detained and tried.
  • Don't talk about yourself with relatives by phone. Agree on a code word and on who will tell you if relatives are detained.
  • You, from abroad, should contact organisations designated 'extremist' or 'undesirable', not your relatives from Russia or Belarus.
  • If your relatives signed a non-disclosure undertaking, don't ask them about the case by phone or in chats: telling you could lead to a case against them. Let your lawyer get the details.

Designations of organisations in this guide: OVD-Info (foreign agent, extremist, on Rosfinmonitoring list); Viasna Human Rights Centre (extremist in Belarus). Contacting them carries a risk: do not write from a phone that may be seized, and do not repost their materials.
