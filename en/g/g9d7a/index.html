Draft What human rights defenders advise

Your home is being searched

  • Ask to see the court order. In urgent cases a search can go ahead without one, with the court notified later (art. 165 pt 5 CPC).
  • The law requires two attesting witnesses (arts 170, 182 CPC). You can ask for neighbours to be called; any refusal should go into the record.
  • Call a lawyer straight away: you have the right to one, but the investigator does not have to wait for them to arrive.
  • The law does not expressly require you to give passwords; human rights groups point to art. 51 of the Constitution. In practice a refusal is sometimes met with pressure, so decide with your lawyer in advance how to handle it. Turn on encryption and a passcode lock in advance. During a search, do not hide or switch off devices in front of officers: it can make your situation worse.
  • Human rights groups advise putting every violation into the comments on the record, asking for a full copy and watching that nothing is planted.
Safety
  • Now: keep your lawyer's number on paper and don't discuss the case, your views or other people in front of officers.
  • Traces: seized devices are examined, and messages stay with the people you wrote to and in cloud backups even if you deleted them yourself.
  • Don't: after a search, delete nothing and don't wipe seized devices remotely without a lawyer: it can be used against you, and deleting won't remove what has already been copied.
