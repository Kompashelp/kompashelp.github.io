Telegram: a 5-minute privacy setup

For everyone using Telegram in Russia, Belarus or Central Asia, or chatting with people there.

The 5-minute minimum

  • In Russia Telegram was restricted from August 2025 and almost fully blocked by 10 April 2026 (Meduza, citing OONI). Keep a way to reach your lawyer and family outside Telegram.
  • Hide your number: Settings → Privacy and Security → Phone Number: 'Nobody' can see it, only 'My Contacts' can find you by it.
  • Turn on Two-Step Verification (cloud password) with a password you use nowhere else.
  • Check Settings → Devices, end unknown sessions and set old sessions to end automatically.
  • Set an app passcode and use auto-delete for sensitive chats.

If you are at risk

  • Forwarded messages: Nobody. Calls → peer-to-peer: Nobody (hides your IP). Invites: My Contacts.
  • Turn off contact sync and delete synced contacts.
  • Use secret chats (not available in every app, e.g. not in Telegram Desktop) for what matters most: they are not stored in Telegram's cloud.
  • For trips to Russia or Belarus use a separate account: signing in to your main one brings back every channel and chat.

What not to do

  • Never share a login code, not even with 'support' or 'rights defenders'.
  • Don't react, send Stars or comment in channels of organisations designated extremist, terrorist or undesirable. Criminal cases have been opened over Stars.
  • Don't install 'improved' Telegram versions from forwarded links.
  • If a case has been opened, a report drawn up or your phone seized, don't delete anything without a lawyer: deletion can be used against you and won't remove what the authorities have already copied.

What traces remain

  • Regular chats, groups, channel lists, reactions and comments are stored on Telegram's servers.
  • Since September 2024 Telegram's privacy policy says it may disclose a suspect's IP address and phone number to authorities on a court order.
