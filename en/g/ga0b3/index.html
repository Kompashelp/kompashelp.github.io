Interpol, surveillance and abductions

  • Check whether Interpol holds a request about you (see 'Am I on a list?'). Most Red Notices are not public, so a full request about yourself and a complaint go to Interpol's Commission for the Control of Files (CCF).
  • Interpol's Constitution (art. 3) bars political cases. According to Interpol's internal data, about half of Russian files reviewed by the CCF did not comply with its rules.
  • CIS-channel searches bypass Interpol's filters. Abductions are known in Kyrgyzstan, Kazakhstan, Armenia, Georgia and Uzbekistan: people were lured to meetings or seized near bases.
  • Rights groups advise not agreeing to 'business' meetings and keeping threats as evidence for the police and a lawyer. Let one trusted person outside Russia know your route. Don't give details to family in Russia: they may be questioned.
  • Digital security: the Access Now helpline (24/7, Russian spoken) and First Department guides.
  • According to the BBC and Disclose (leaked Interpol documents, 26.01.2026), Russian requests drew three times as many complaints as those of Turkey, the runner-up: at least 700 people wanted by Moscow complained to the CCF, and more than 400 had their notices cancelled. Yet in 2024 about 90% of Russian requests still passed the initial check, so Interpol's filter does not always work.
Safety
  • Now: keep copies of documents on the political nature of the case and on threats off your phone, with a trusted person or a lawyer.
  • Traces: the authorities see requests for certificates made via Gosuslugi, and posts showing where you live help others find you.
  • Don't: if there is a case against you, don't plan transfers in countries that extradite or deport people to Russia, and don't fly with Russian airlines.
