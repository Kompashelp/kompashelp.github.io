Officers ask your relatives to sign something: what it is and what to do
  • First find out what the paper is. Ask for the officer's name, position and agency. Police must state their position, rank and surname and show ID on request (art. 5, Law on Police). Ask whether it is an explanation, questioning, inspection, search or warning: that decides whether a signature is required. A 'chat', an 'interview' and an 'explanation' are not mandatory by law (OVD-Info).
  • A record of questioning or a search. A signature confirms the record is accurate. You can refuse to sign and this is noted (art. 167 CCP), but rights groups more often advise signing with remarks. Read everything, demand corrections, write remarks on separate sheets, cross out blank spaces, take a copy. Don't sign consent to be notified by text message.
  • One line for the record. Kompas wording, agree it with a lawyer: 'I refuse to testify against my son (daughter, husband) under art. 51 of the Constitution of the Russian Federation.' In Belarus: '...under art. 27 of the Constitution of the Republic of Belarus.' Refuse question by question rather than 'all questions': First Department warns the latter risks a case for refusing to testify.
  • Consent to inspect the flat. A home may be inspected only with the residents' consent or a court decision (arts. 12, 177 CCP). Without a court decision you don't have to sign consent. An operational 'survey' also needs consent (OVD-Info). A search under a court decision or an urgent order cannot be stopped. In Belarus a prosecutor, not a court, authorises it (arts. 204, 210 CCP).
  • Non-disclosure and warnings. After a non-disclosure undertaking relatives must not pass on case data, including to you (art. 310 Criminal Code; Belarus art. 407). Rights groups disagree on whether it can be refused, but refusing does not protect from liability. A warning counts as issued even without a signature. Keep your copy: it can be appealed, to a court within three months (OVD-Info).
  • Summonses, videos, property, 'cooperation'. Relatives can be handed a summons to questioning for you, but not a military summons. No law obliges them to write letters, record videos, hand over keys or sign 'consent to cooperate'. Property is frozen by court decision with a record (art. 115 CCP). First Department says refusing recruitment has no legal consequences.
  • Recording and lawyers. The law does not ban audio-recording questioning, but filming in a police building or during a search needs permission (First Department). You can ask for an official recording (art. 189 CCP). A witness may bring a lawyer. Officers need not wait for a lawyer during a search, so call one before opening the door.
Safety
  • How to tell you. Relatives can photograph copies of the papers and pass them to your lawyer rather than to you on Telegram or WhatsApp: First Department advises against discussing such matters there. If they signed a non-disclosure undertaking, let the lawyer get the case details.
  • Don't come back because of papers your relatives signed, and don't answer officers through your relatives. Decide on any response with a lawyer.
  • Relatives in Russia and Belarus should not write to organisations designated 'extremist' or 'undesirable'. You write from abroad.

Designations of organisations in this guide: OVD-Info (foreign agent, extremist, on Rosfinmonitoring list). Contacting them carries a risk: do not write from a phone that may be seized, and do not repost their materials.
