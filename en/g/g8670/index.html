Google and Apple accounts: cloud, location, Find My

For iPhone and Android users whose Apple or Google account holds photos, chats, phone backups and location history.

The 5-minute minimum

  • The cloud keeps a copy of your phone: data deleted on the phone often survives in the backup. iPhone: Settings → [your name] → iCloud. Android: Settings → Google → All services → Backup.
  • Deleted photos stay 30 days in Recently Deleted on iPhone and up to 60 days in Google Photos trash.
  • Turn on two-factor authentication and review signed-in devices (Apple: Settings → [your name] → Devices; Google: myaccount.google.com/security-checkup).
  • Location history: iPhone Significant Locations & Routes; Android Google Maps → profile → Timeline. Check who you share your location with.

If you are at risk

  • iPhone: Advanced Data Protection end-to-end encrypts backups, photos and notes.
  • iPhone Safety Check shows who has access to your location and data and lets you revoke it at once.
  • Find My and Find Hub can erase a lost phone, but for a seized phone only after talking to a lawyer.

What not to do

  • Don't sign in to your main Apple or Google account on a travel phone.
  • Don't remotely wipe a seized phone or laptop (Find My, Find Hub) without talking to a lawyer first: it can be treated as destroying evidence.
  • If a case has been opened, a report drawn up or your phone seized, don't delete anything without a lawyer: deletion can be used against you and won't remove what the authorities have already copied.

What traces remain

  • Anyone holding your unlocked phone can see your cloud too.
  • Companies hand over data on lawful requests; what they give Russian authorities is not publicly known.
