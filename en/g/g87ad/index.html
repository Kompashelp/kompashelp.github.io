Draft Safety

SIM card, phone number, Gosuslugi, banks

For anyone with a Russian number linked to Gosuslugi, banks and messengers, in Russia or after leaving.

The 5-minute minimum

  • A Russian number is registered to your passport, so it identifies the account owner. Hide it in Telegram and on social media.
  • Where possible, use a password and an authenticator app for login codes instead of SMS codes: SMS messages get intercepted, and a SIM card can be reissued.
  • Don't hand your SIM card, bank card or account access to other people, and don't use other people's. Since July 2025 passing on bank cards and account access is a criminal offence (art. 187 of the Criminal Code), and since 1 September 2025 you may pass your SIM card only to close relatives. Someone else's SIM does not protect you.
  • Never tell anyone an SMS code: not a 'bank employee', not 'Gosuslugi', not the 'FSB' on the phone. Real staff never ask for codes.

If you are at risk

  • For accounts linked to civic activity, use your own foreign number and email on a foreign service, not Russian ones.
  • Gosuslugi: every login, IP address and device is recorded and visible to the state. You can see your own login history in your profile, in the security section.
  • Banks see transfers, recipients and payment comments and pass the information to the security services and Rosfinmonitoring. Donations to designated organisations from a Russian card leave a direct trace.
  • If you have left: the operator may end the contract if the number is not used or paid for over 6 months, and each operator has its own rules. Since November 2025, after you return from roaming or after 72 hours without activity, mobile internet and SMS on a Russian SIM card can be blocked for 24 hours; you can lift the block via the link in the operator's SMS or by calling the operator.
  • Since 4 August 2026 a law on a register of people who have left and are 'evading punishment' has been in force (a sentence or a 'political' ruling that has not been carried out, plus living abroad). According to OVD-Info ('extremist', 'foreign agent'), people on the register lose access to online Gosuslugi and to transfers through Russian banks' apps.
  • If your SIM card was seized together with the phone, restore the number at the operator with your passport so that nobody can receive login codes on it. If there is a case, discuss this with a lawyer first.

What not to do

  • Don't use other people's SIM cards, documents or Gosuslugi accounts, and don't lend your own.
  • Don't buy SIM cards 'without a passport' from strangers.
  • Don't keep screenshots and SMS about transfers to designated organisations on your phone.
  • If a case has been opened, a report drawn up or your phone seized, don't delete anything without a lawyer: deletion can be used against you and won't remove what the authorities have already copied.

What traces remain

  • The operator keeps records of whom you called and texted, when, and where the phone was: operators keep this for 3 years. Since 1 January 2026 Russian internet services on the register of 'information dissemination organisers' must keep data about messages and users for as long (previously 1 year).
  • Logging in to Gosuslugi leaves the state a record with your IP address and the time.
  • Bank transactions are kept for years and are available to the security services on request.
  • Donor data also leaks from the fundraisers themselves: in August 2026 a large fundraiser for political prisoners leaked donors' email addresses and the last digits of their cards.
