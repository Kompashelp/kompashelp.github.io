SIM card, phone number, Gosuslugi, banks

For anyone with a Russian number linked to Gosuslugi, banks and messengers, in Russia or after leaving.

The 5-minute minimum

  • A Russian number is registered to your passport, so it identifies the account owner. Hide it in Telegram and on social media.
  • Where possible use a password plus an authenticator app instead of SMS codes.
  • Never hand your SIM card, bank card or account access to others and never use other people's: Russia has made passing on cards and accounts a crime, and someone else's SIM does not protect you.
  • Never tell anyone an SMS code, whatever they claim to be.

If you are at risk

  • For accounts linked to civic activity use your own foreign number and a foreign email provider.
  • Every Gosuslugi login, IP address and device is recorded and visible to the state.
  • Banks see transfers, recipients and payment comments and share them with the security services and Rosfinmonitoring.

What not to do

  • Don't use or lend SIM cards, documents or Gosuslugi accounts.
  • If a case has been opened, a report drawn up or your phone seized, don't delete anything without a lawyer: deletion can be used against you and won't remove what the authorities have already copied.

What traces remain

  • Operators keep call and message records and location data for 3 years; since 1 January 2026 Russian internet services on the 'information dissemination organisers' register must keep message metadata and user data for 3 years too (previously 1). Banks keep transactions for years.
