Safety

Short answers to the questions people ask most before a border crossing, after a detention or a hack. Each guide has three levels: a 5-minute minimum, what to do if you are at risk, and what not to do. Every tip lowers the risk but does not remove it.

If you need help now
  • Phone seized, account hacked or surveillance suspected: contact the Access Now helpline. Free, 24/7, Russian spoken.
  • Human rights defenders and journalists at risk: Front Line Defenders' 24/7 emergency line, Russian spoken.
  • Facing a case over a post, repost or comment: Net Freedoms (@nfp_hotline) or First Department (@deptonebot, foreign agent).

General rules

  • To delete or not: while there is no case, remove risky content well in advance. If a case has been opened, a report drawn up or your phone seized, don't delete anything without a lawyer: deletion can be used against you and won't remove what the authorities have already copied.
  • Don't remotely wipe a seized phone or laptop (Find My, Find Hub) without talking to a lawyer first: it can be treated as destroying evidence.
  • Only your own SIM cards, documents and accounts. Other people's don't protect you and create new risks.
  • The main threat is usually not hacking but your own phone in someone else's hands.
  • We don't name VPN services or link to them.
  • Links lead to detailed guides by the organisations; their designation in Russia or Belarus is shown next to each link. Read designated organisations' materials, but don't share them.
  • Apps change. If a menu item has a different name, use the search in Settings. Paths checked for iOS 26, Android 16 (Pixel) and Telegram as of 26.09.2026.

Reading this site without leaving traces

  • The 'Exit' button (or the Esc key on a computer) immediately replaces this page with a neutral website. Your browser history stays, though. You need to clear it separately.
  • iPhone (Safari): Settings → Apps → Safari → 'Clear History and Website Data'.
  • Android (Chrome): menu ⋮ → 'History' → 'Delete browsing data'.
  • Computer: Ctrl+Shift+Delete (Cmd+Shift+Delete on a Mac) opens history clearing in most browsers.
  • It is safer to open the site in an 'Incognito' or 'Private' tab: once it is closed, the browser keeps no history or cookies. Your internet provider, employer or network owner can still see that you visited the site.
  • Don't open Kompas in Yandex Browser, the VK browser or through Yandex search. These companies are required to hand data to the authorities.
  • If you are signed in to Chrome with a Google account, your history is also saved in the account. Delete it at myactivity.google.com; clearing the phone is not enough.
  • When you send a link to this site in a messenger, the other person sees a preview. It stays on their phone. Only forward links to people you trust.
  • A downloaded PDF sits in 'Downloads' under an obvious name. Rename it or delete it after reading.
  • Your phone keyboard remembers what you type. Avoid typing organisations' names on your main phone unless you need to.

Your phone

  • Lock your phone with a long passcode and turn off face and fingerprint unlock.
  • Switch your phone off before a border or a protest. After a restart it is best protected until you enter the passcode.
  • A completely empty phone may raise questions at the border. For travel an ordinary phone with a safe history is better, prepared a few weeks in advance. If there was no time, an empty phone is still better than your main one.
  • Deleted data can often be restored from the cloud. Check your iCloud, Google and Telegram backups.
  • Meduza's detailed guide to your phone at the Russian border: https://meduza.io/en/feature/2026/08/29/how-to-prepare-your-mobile-phone-for-the-russian-border-meduza-s-complete-survival-guide
  • Meduza is designated 'undesirable' in Russia: open the link through a VPN and do not share it.

Telegram and messengers

  • WhatsApp, Signal and Viber are blocked in Russia. Since April 2026 Telegram has been almost completely blocked in Russia and usually does not open without a VPN. Most contacts in this directory lead to Telegram.
  • In Telegram's privacy settings, hide your number and stop people finding you by it.
  • Turn on the cloud password and check the list of active sessions once a month.
  • Use auto-delete for sensitive conversations.
  • Leave chats where people know your real name if dangerous topics are discussed there.
  • Keep a backup channel outside Telegram: email, Delta Chat, a number on paper. Many organisations in this directory list one under 'If Telegram doesn't open'.
  • The state messenger MAX has no end-to-end encryption. Don't write anything important on it and don't use it to contact human rights groups.
  • Do not share material from organisations designated 'extremist' or 'undesirable': people face administrative and criminal cases for this. Reading something is not the same as spreading it.
  • Subscriptions and likes are visible if your phone is inspected and sometimes feature in cases. Remove them well before travelling to Russia.

VPN

  • By law using a VPN is not banned. In practice people are fined for deliberately searching for material on the extremist list (art. 13.53 CAO).
  • Don't forward links to VPNs or VPN bots: this can be treated as advertising tools for bypassing blocks (art. 14.3 CAO), and people are fined for it.
  • We do not recommend specific services or link to them. Guides from First Line (firstlinehelp.info), EFF (ssd.eff.org/ru) and Access Now can help you choose.
  • Blocking comes in waves. Keep a backup option and update it in advance, not when the shutdown hits.

Money

  • Your bank and the recipient see transfers, Telegram 'stars' and payment comments.
  • Check an organisation's designation before donating. It is shown on its card.
  • If you travel to Russia, don't pay designated organisations with a Russian card.

People around you

  • Agree a signal word and a check-in call with the people close to you.
  • Don't keep your devices at your relatives' homes.
  • Remember that pressure can be put on your family. Decide together in advance what they will say if someone comes to them.
  • Don't agree to meet strangers 'about your case': people have been lured this way and, abroad, abducted. If a meeting is unavoidable, choose a busy place and tell a friend where you are.

If you are being followed or threatened

  • Write down dates, places and descriptions, and keep any threats.
  • Contact Access Now: its digital security helpline works around the clock, including in Russian. https://www.accessnow.org/help/

Look after yourself

  • Tiredness and fear make it harder to think about safety.
  • If things are hard, write to First Line or Nora.

Spotting the official bot or channel

  • Popular projects have many clones that harvest data. Take the bot link from the organisation's official website, not from forwarded messages.
  • Example: Get Lost (Idite Lesom) has only one official bot: @iditelesom_info_bot.
  • Check the username character by character: clones differ by a single letter, underscore or digit.
  • Genuine human rights groups never ask for passwords, SMS codes or payment for advice in a chat.
